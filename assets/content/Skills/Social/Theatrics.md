Theatrics is playing a role. The other persuasion skills project the character's own person at a listener, however manipulatively; Theatrics explicitly presents somebody else. Even the untrained can make an awkward attempt at it. Doing it well demands invention and vocal command both.

The skill covers three related tasks:

**Acting** — taking on another personality, real or invented. It may involve mimicry and disguise, but need not.

**Mimicry** — altering the voice to sound like something else: another accent, another person, a bird's call.

**Disguise** — altering the appearance. Difficulty scales sharply with specificity, from **+20** for looking like a generic member of some class or trade to **−40** for impersonating a particular named individual whom the audience knows by sight.

**Layering.** When a character uses one of the other persuasion skills from inside an assumed role, that other skill is the one tested, and Theatrics applies only its Secondary Modifier. Walking past a sceptical guard in a stolen tabard tests Theatrics alone; standing there lying to him tests **Guile (Theatrics)**.

**Opposition.** Against an idle target, Theatrics is unopposed. An alert target resists a physical disguise with **Awareness**, at 0 to −20 for distance; if they can talk to the impostor, they oppose with **Intrigue** against Theatrics to test whether the role holds up, ties among successes broken in the target's favour.

**Artistry.** Not every use is manipulation. A performance's aesthetic worth is a plain Theatrics Success Value test, its Value Diamonds measuring the impact on the audience exactly as Dancing does.
