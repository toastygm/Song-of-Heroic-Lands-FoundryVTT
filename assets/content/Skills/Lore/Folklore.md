Folklore is what an educated person of a realm knows about that realm — traditionally parcelled out among six disciplines: geography, history, the nature of the world, mythic beasts, poetics and society. It is the skill that answers "have I heard of this?", and it covers a very great deal of ground because in a world without reference libraries, one well-read head is the reference library.

**Recalling something specific** — a name, a date, a custom, which family holds which honour — is a plain Folklore test, and the success level sets how much comes back:

| CF      | MF         | MS      | CS       |
| ------- | ---------- | ------- | -------- |
| Unknown | Incomplete | General | Specific |

**Measuring what a character broadly knows** about a topic is a Folklore Success Value test instead, read on the standard table. The distinction is worth keeping: _who was king during the burning?_ is a Success Test, while _what was the burning?_ is a Success Value test, and the Value Diamonds tell the GM how much of the story to hand over. SV 1–2 is bare recognition of the name; higher results open the subject up.

This is the general pattern for **knowledge Success Value tests**, and any skill whose subject is a body of learning may be read the same way when the four-way success level would be too blunt an instrument.

**Foreign knowledge.** A character may know something of somewhere else, at a penalty scaling with distance:

| Distance                                   | ML  |
| ------------------------------------------ | --- |
| A different realm within the native region | −10 |
| An adjacent region                         | −20 |
| A distant region of the same continent     | −40 |
| Beyond the continent                       | −60 |

Where the reduction takes Folklore Mastery Level below zero, no test may be made — the character simply has nothing to draw on. Extended travel in a particular country mitigates the penalty, as does access to the right libraries and the right tutors. The same scale governs foreign heraldry and foreign law.
