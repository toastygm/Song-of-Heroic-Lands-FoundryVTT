Embalming is the preparation of a corpse for burial according to the observances of a particular faith — evisceration, immersion in spirits, wrapping, and the packing of mouth, nose and ears with herbs, spices and cotton. It is priestly work as often as it is a trade, and the techniques differ between religions in ways their practitioners consider anything but cosmetic.

A body is described by five levels of decomposition, and the pace depends on climate.

| Level   | Condition                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Minimal | Some skin slippage and hair loss.                |
| Slight  | Grey-green discoloration, bloating.              |
| Partial | Brown to black, flesh caving, some bone exposed. |
| Most    | Advanced decay, most bone exposed.               |
| Whole   | Dry bone; a skeleton.                            |

| Level   | Subpolar | Cool temp. | Warm temp. | Subtropical | Dry tropical | Wet tropical |
| ------- | -------- | ---------- | ---------- | ----------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| Minimal | 7 days   | 6 days     | 5 days     | 4 days      | 3 days       | 2 days       |
| Slight  | 10 days  | 9 days     | 7 days     | 6 days      | 4 days       | 3 days       |
| Partial | 45 days  | 35 days    | 30 days    | 20 days     | 15 days      | 15 days      |
| Most    | 1 year   | 6 months   | 5 months   | 4 months    | 3 months     | 3 months     |
| Whole   | 4 years  | 3 years    | 2 years    | 1½ years    | 1 year       | 1 year       |

**Preservation.** An embalmer extends each of those thresholds by a flat number of days equal to the Success Value of an Embalming Success Value test — an SV 8 treatment simply adds eight days at every stage.

**Forensics.** A successful Embalming test may draw conclusions from a body. The results are nothing like modern certainty, but cause and rough time of death are often recoverable.
