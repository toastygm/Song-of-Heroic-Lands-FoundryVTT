Mineralogy is practical earth-knowledge: simple chemistry, geology, prospecting, the working of a mine and the refining of what comes out of it. A character may test Mineralogy to identify an inorganic substance, judge a seam, or price a rough stone. The skill frequently serves as a Secondary Modifier to others — an alchemist's preparations and a weaponsmith's steel both depend on knowing what the material actually is.

**Mixtures.** Exotic preparations made with Mineralogy are mixtures, and the two best known are both thrown weapons.

A **fire pot** is a glass jar of pitch, sulphur, resin and oil, lit and thrown by hand or slung with a staff sling. The jar breaks on landing and floods a five-foot circle with fire; anything in that space takes an area attack of **d4 + 4** fire impact. It costs **12d** to make.

A **quicklime pot** is a two-quart ceramic pot of burnt limestone. It bursts into a ten-foot cloud of caustic powder, and targets caught inside are incapacitated by choking and blindness, with a shock reroll **2d3 rounds** later. Usually lobbed by a light engine, though a strong arm will manage a short throw. It costs **24d** to make.

Making either takes **one day**, in quantities limited chiefly by money. The maker then makes a Mineralogy Success Value test, and **the Success Value itself becomes the Target Number** for the pot's later use. Prepared mixtures keep indefinitely, though rough handling may spoil or set them off — a successful d10 roll against that Target Number avoids the mishap. When one is finally thrown, a d10 is rolled against the Target Number: on a failure, a fire pot manages only **d4 + 2** impact and a quicklime pot allows its shock reroll after only **d3** rounds. A pot made on a Critical Failure test is simply a dud if that final roll fails.
