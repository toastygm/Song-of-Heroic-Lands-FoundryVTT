Language is fluency and comprehension in one particular tongue; each language is a separate skill with its own Mastery Level.

**Complexity.** Every native speaker is technically fluent, so Mastery Level does not measure whether a character can speak but _how far_ they can go — what ideas they can express and follow, in their own tongue as much as a foreign one. When two people converse, the **lesser of their two Indexes** sets the ceiling:

| Index | Complexity | What it supports                                                                   |
| ----- | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–2   | Limited    | Simple questions and answers; narrow vocabulary; halting or ungrammatical speech.  |
| 3–5   | Basic      | Routine social exchange; unspecialised vocabulary. The average native speaker.     |
| 6–7   | Advanced   | Intricate social exchange; specialised vocabulary. The educated and the well-born. |
| 8+    | Profound   | Erudite, detailed, and equal to any concept. The scholar, the jurist, the herald.  |

That ceiling limits persuasion as much as conversation. A character with Limited fluency may still lie, but only in short, plain sentences — and being reduced to short, plain sentences is itself conspicuous.

**Mutual intelligibility.** Some languages are close enough that a speaker of one can make headway in the other, and related tongues should be treated as partially available rather than closed.

**Accent.** How foreign a character sounds is the product of their **Eloquence Index × Language Index**:

| Total | Accent     |
| ----- | ---------- |
| 01–19 | Heavy      |
| 20–29 | Noticeable |
| 30–39 | Slight     |
| 40+   | None       |

Because it is a product, neither factor saves the other: a naturally eloquent speaker with a weak grasp of the language is still obviously foreign, and years of study will move an accent from heavy to slight without ever quite erasing it.

**Reading** requires a Script skill as well as this one — see Script for the reading and scribing tests, in which Language supplies the comprehension half.
