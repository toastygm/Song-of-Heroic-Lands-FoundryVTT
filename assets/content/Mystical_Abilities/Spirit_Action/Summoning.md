Summoning calls an entity from beyond the physical world — a spirit, an elemental force, or something worse — and compels or bargains it into service. It is two acts, not one, and the second is the hard part: bringing a thing across is a matter of the correct invocation, while holding it to terms once it has arrived is a matter of what the summoner brought to the bargain.

It is the most dangerous of the spirit disciplines for exactly that reason. What answers a summons may be hostile, capricious, or far stronger than the one who called it, and it need not announce which. A summoner who cannot bind what they have called has not made a mistake in a roll; they have introduced a free and unfriendly power into the room.

**Invoking it.** A summoning is a [[doc-sccsstst#success-test|Success Test]] like any other [[doc-mystabilintro|Mystical Ability]], and it is **spirit-governed**: it draws its [[doc-mstrylvl#effective-mastery-level|EML]] from the [[doc-sprtpwr|Spirit Power]] it is performed through rather than from a skill of its own, so there is no summoning without a spirit whose conduit the summoner already holds. A summoned entity that is not simply obedient is dealt with as an [[doc-oppsdtst|opposed test]] of the summoner's command against the entity's own resistance.

**What bears on the test.** The Spirit Power the summons works through; the preparation, the place, and whatever the summoner offers or threatens; and the standing and nature of the thing called, since a greater entity is harder both to call and to hold. Nothing may be summoned while the character carries [[doc-arlshck|Aural Shock]], and no Mystical Ability test may be [[doc-fatepnts#fate-exclusions|fated]] — the bargain is struck on its own terms, and luck gets no say in whether it answers.

The form is set out under [[doc-sprtactn|Spirit Action]], and the channel it works through under [[doc-sprtpwr|Spirit Power]].
